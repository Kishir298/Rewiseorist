# Forces

## What is a force?

A force is a push or a pull that can change an object's motion, shape or size. Force is a **vector**, which means it has both size and direction. The unit of force is the **newton (N)**. On diagrams, arrows show forces: a longer arrow represents a larger force, and the arrow points in the direction of the force.

Forces can be **contact forces**, such as friction, air resistance, tension and the normal contact force, or **non-contact forces**, such as gravity, magnetism and electrostatic force.

## Resultant forces

When several forces act on an object, we can add them to find the **resultant force**. Forces acting in the same direction add together. Forces acting in opposite directions subtract.

- If the resultant force is zero, the forces are **balanced**. The object stays still or moves at a constant velocity.
- If the resultant force is not zero, the forces are **unbalanced**. The object accelerates, decelerates or changes direction.

## Newton's laws

Newton's first law says that an object stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it. This is why a moving object does not need a continuous force to keep moving at a steady speed.

Newton's second law links force, mass and acceleration:

**F = ma**

where F is the resultant force in newtons, m is the mass in kilograms and a is the acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²).

Newton's third law says that if object A exerts a force on object B, then B exerts an equal and opposite force on A. These two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel out.

## Weight and mass

**Mass** is the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms. It does not change if the object moves to another planet. **Weight** is the force of gravity on an object, measured in newtons. The two are linked by:

**W = mg**

On Earth, the gravitational field strength g is about 9.8 N/kg.

## Worked example

A 5 kg box is pushed to the right with 20 N while friction acts to the left with 8 N.

Resultant force = 20 N − 8 N = 12 N to the right.

Acceleration = F ÷ m = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4 m/s².

The weight of the box is W = mg = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N.

## Friction and terminal velocity

Friction opposes motion and transfers energy to the thermal store of the surroundings. Air resistance is a type of friction that increases with speed. A falling object reaches **terminal velocity** when its weight is balanced by air resistance, so the resultant force becomes zero and it falls at a steady speed.

## Key facts

- Force is a vector measured in newtons; arrows show its size and direction.
- Balanced forces give a zero resultant force and no change in velocity.
- Newton's second law is F = ma, where force is in newtons and mass in kilograms.
- Weight is the force of gravity, W = mg, while mass is the amount of matter.
- At terminal velocity, weight and air resistance are equal, so acceleration is zero.
