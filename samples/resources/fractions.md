# Fractions

## What a fraction is

A fraction describes equal parts of a whole. In the fraction **a/b**, the **denominator** (b) tells you how many equal parts the whole is split into, and the **numerator** (a) tells you how many of those parts you have. For example, 3/8 means three of eight equal slices.

**Equivalent fractions** look different but have the same value. Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same number: 2/3 = 8/12. To simplify, divide both parts by their highest common factor (HCF). For 18/24 the HCF is 6, so 18/24 = 3/4.

A **proper fraction** has a smaller numerator than denominator. An **improper fraction** has a numerator that is equal to or larger than the denominator. A **mixed number** combines a whole number and a fraction, such as 2 1/2.

## Comparing fractions

Give fractions a common denominator, then compare numerators. To compare 3/4 and 5/7, use 28: 3/4 = 21/28 and 5/7 = 20/28. Therefore 3/4 is larger. Cross-multiplication works too: compare 3 × 7 with 5 × 4.

## Adding and subtracting

Fractions need the same denominator before they can be added or subtracted.

Worked example: 2/3 + 1/4.
The lowest common denominator is 12.
2/3 = 8/12 and 1/4 = 3/12.
8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12.

For mixed numbers, either convert to improper fractions or handle wholes and parts separately. Example: 1 1/2 + 2 3/4 = 3/2 + 11/4 = 6/4 + 11/4 = 17/4 = 4 1/4.

## Multiplying and dividing

To multiply, multiply numerators together and denominators together. Cancelling first keeps the numbers small: 3/5 × 10/9 = (3 × 10)/(5 × 9) = 30/45 = 2/3.

To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction (flip it).

Worked example: 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1 7/8.

## Fractions, decimals and percentages

Divide the numerator by the denominator to get a decimal: 3/8 = 0.375. Multiply by 100 for a percentage: 37.5%. To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator. So 2/5 of 60 = 60 ÷ 5 × 2 = 24.

## Key facts

- The denominator shows how many equal parts make a whole; the numerator counts the parts taken.
- Simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by their HCF.
- Add and subtract only after writing the fractions with a common denominator.
- To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.
- Convert a fraction to a decimal by division, then to a percentage by multiplying the decimal by 100.
