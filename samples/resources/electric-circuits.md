# Electric circuits

## Charge and current

**Electric current** is the rate of flow of electric charge. It is measured in **amperes (A)**, often called amps. Conventional current flows from the positive terminal of a cell to the negative terminal, even though electrons move the other way.

The equation linking charge, current and time is:

**Q = It**

where Q is charge in coulombs (C), I is current in amps and t is time in seconds.

Worked example: a current of 2 A flows for 30 s.
Q = 2 × 30 = 60 C.

## Potential difference

**Potential difference** (p.d.), or voltage, is the energy transferred per unit of charge. It is measured in **volts (V)**.

**V = W / Q**

where W is energy in joules and Q is charge in coulombs. A 6 V battery transfers 6 J of energy to each coulomb of charge that passes through it.

## Resistance and Ohm's law

**Resistance** measures how difficult it is for current to flow. It is measured in **ohms (Ω)**.

**V = IR** and **R = V / I**.

A resistor obeys Ohm's law if its resistance stays constant when its temperature is constant. A filament lamp does not obey Ohm's law: as it heats up, its resistance increases. A diode conducts in one direction only and has a very high resistance in the reverse direction.

## Series circuits

In a series circuit there is only one path for the current.

- The current is the same at every point.
- The potential difference of the supply is shared between components.
- The total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances: R = R₁ + R₂ + ...

If one component breaks, the circuit is incomplete and nothing works.

## Parallel circuits

In a parallel circuit, components are connected across the same two points.

- The potential difference across each branch is the same as the supply voltage.
- The current from the supply splits between the branches.
- The total resistance is less than the resistance of the smallest branch.

Each branch works independently, so one broken branch does not stop the others. This is why household lighting is wired in parallel.

## Power and energy

**Power** is the rate of energy transfer, measured in watts (W).

**P = IV** and **P = I²R**.

**E = Pt** gives energy transferred in joules when power is in watts and time is in seconds.

Worked example: a 12 V supply drives a current of 3 A through a motor.
Power = 12 × 3 = 36 W.
In 10 s the energy transferred is E = 36 × 10 = 360 J.

## Key facts

- Current is the rate of flow of charge, measured in amps, and Q = It.
- Potential difference is energy transferred per coulomb, measured in volts.
- Resistance is measured in ohms and is calculated using R = V / I.
- In series, current is the same everywhere and voltages add; in parallel, voltage is the same across branches and current splits.
- Electrical power is P = IV, and energy transferred is E = Pt.
- A filament lamp's resistance increases as its temperature rises, so it is not an ohmic conductor.
