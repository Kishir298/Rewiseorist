# Indices

## What an index means

An **index** (plural: indices) is another name for a power. In aⁿ, the number a is the **base** and n is the **index** or exponent. The index tells you how many times to multiply the base by itself:

2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16.

When a term has a coefficient, the index applies only to the letter unless brackets say otherwise. In 3x², only x is squared: 3x² means 3 × x × x, not (3x)².

## The laws of indices

The laws work when the bases are the same.

- **Multiplying:** add the indices. aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ. Example: 2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷ = 128.
- **Dividing:** subtract the indices. aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ. Example: 5⁷ ÷ 5⁴ = 5³ = 125.
- **Power of a power:** multiply the indices. (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ. Example: (3²)⁴ = 3⁸.
- **Zero index:** any non-zero number to the power zero equals 1. Example: 7⁰ = 1.
- **Negative index:** take the reciprocal. a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ. Example: 4⁻² = 1/4² = 1/16.

## Fractional indices

A fractional index represents a root. The denominator tells you which root to take, and the numerator tells you the power:

a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a and a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ.

Worked example: 16^(3/4).
First find the fourth root: 16^(1/4) = 2, because 2⁴ = 16.
Then cube the result: 2³ = 8.
So 16^(3/4) = 8.

Another example: 27^(1/3) = 3, because 3³ = 27.

## Brackets and coefficients

When a whole term is raised to a power, every part inside the brackets is raised to that power.

(2x³)² = 2² × (x³)² = 4x⁶.

(3a²b)² = 9a⁴b².

## Standard form

Standard form writes numbers as a × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer. For example, 4.5 × 10³ = 4500, and 0.00062 = 6.2 × 10⁻⁴.

To multiply numbers in standard form, multiply the first parts and add the powers of 10. For example:

(2 × 10³) × (3 × 10⁵) = 6 × 10⁸.

If the first part is not between 1 and 10, adjust it and change the power. For example, 12 × 10⁴ = 1.2 × 10⁵.

## Key facts

- An index shows repeated multiplication of the same base.
- When multiplying powers of the same base, add the indices; when dividing, subtract them.
- A power raised to a power has its indices multiplied.
- A negative index means the reciprocal of the positive power.
- A fractional index means a root: the denominator is the root and the numerator is the power.
- Standard form is a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10.
