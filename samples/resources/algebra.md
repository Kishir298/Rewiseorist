# Algebra

## The language of algebra

Algebra uses letters to represent numbers that may be unknown or may vary. A **variable** is a letter standing for a value. An **expression** is a combination of numbers, variables and operations, such as 3x + 5. An **equation** says two expressions are equal, such as 3x + 5 = 20. A **formula** is a rule connecting variables, such as A = lw. An **identity** is true for every possible value, and is written with ≡.

In a term such as 7x, the number 7 is the **coefficient** and x is the variable. Terms with exactly the same variable part are called **like terms**.

## Simplifying expressions

Collect like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients.

7x + 3y − 2x + y = (7x − 2x) + (3y + y) = 5x + 4y.

When multiplying, multiply the numbers and add the indices of the same letter:

3a × 4a² = 12a³.

## Expanding brackets

Multiply every term inside the bracket by the term outside.

4(2x − 3) = 8x − 12.

For two brackets, multiply each term in the first by each term in the second.

Worked example: (x + 3)(x − 4).
x(x − 4) = x² − 4x.
3(x − 4) = 3x − 12.
Add: x² − 4x + 3x − 12 = x² − x − 12.

## Factorising

Factorising is the reverse of expanding. Always look for a common factor first.

6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3).

For a quadratic such as x² + 5x + 6, look for two numbers that multiply to give 6 and add to give 5. These are 2 and 3, so:

x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).

A difference of two squares factorises as a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b), so x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3).

## Solving linear equations

Keep the equation balanced by doing the same operation to both sides.

Worked example: solve 5x − 7 = 18.
Add 7 to both sides: 5x = 25.
Divide by 5: x = 5.
Check: 5 × 5 − 7 = 18, which is correct.

If brackets are involved, expand first. To solve 4(x − 3) = 2x + 6:
4x − 12 = 2x + 6.
Subtract 2x: 2x − 12 = 6.
Add 12: 2x = 18.
Divide by 2: x = 9.

## Solving quadratic equations

Rearrange the equation so one side is zero, factorise, then set each bracket equal to zero.

x² + 5x + 6 = 0 gives (x + 2)(x + 3) = 0, so x = −2 or x = −3.

## Substitution and rearranging

Substitute by replacing a letter with a value. If x = 4, then 3x + 5 = 3 × 4 + 5 = 17.

To rearrange a formula, use inverse operations. From v = u + at, subtract u and divide by t to get a = (v − u) / t.

## Key facts

- Collect like terms by combining their coefficients; the variable part stays the same.
- Expand brackets by multiplying every term inside by the term outside.
- Factorising reverses expanding; check for a common factor first.
- Solve equations by doing the same operation to both sides to keep them balanced.
- For a quadratic equation, rearrange to zero, factorise, and set each bracket to zero.
- Substitute values carefully, and rearrange formulae using inverse operations.
