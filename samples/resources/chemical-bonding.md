# Chemical bonding

## Why atoms bond

Atoms are most stable when they have a full outer electron shell, like the noble gases. Most atoms do not have a full shell on their own, so they bond with other atoms to gain, lose or share electrons. The three main types of bonding are ionic, covalent and metallic.

## Ionic bonding

Ionic bonding happens between a metal and a non-metal. Electrons are **transferred** from the metal atom to the non-metal atom, producing charged particles called **ions**. Metals lose electrons and become positive ions; non-metals gain electrons and become negative ions. The oppositely charged ions are held together by strong **electrostatic attraction**.

In sodium chloride, a sodium atom loses one electron to become Na⁺, and a chlorine atom gains one to become Cl⁻. The ions combine in a 1:1 ratio, so the formula is NaCl. Magnesium oxide contains Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions, so its formula is MgO.

Ionic compounds form a **giant ionic lattice**. The strong attractions between ions give high melting and boiling points. Solid ionic compounds do not conduct electricity because their ions are fixed in place. When melted or dissolved in water, the ions are free to move, so the compound conducts.

## Covalent bonding

Covalent bonding happens between non-metal atoms. The atoms **share pairs of electrons** so that each atom gains a full outer shell. One shared pair is a single bond; two shared pairs make a double bond.

Simple molecular substances, such as water (H₂O) and carbon dioxide (CO₂), have weak forces between their molecules. These weak intermolecular forces mean low melting and boiling points. They do not conduct electricity because they have no free charged particles.

Some covalent substances form **giant covalent structures**. In diamond, each carbon atom is covalently bonded to four others in a rigid network, making it very hard with a high melting point. Graphite also has a giant structure, but each carbon atom bonds to only three others. The layers can slide, and the delocalised electrons allow graphite to conduct electricity.

## Metallic bonding

Metals form a lattice of positive ions surrounded by **delocalised electrons**. The attraction between the ions and the shared electrons holds the structure together. Metals conduct electricity and heat because the delocalised electrons can move. They are malleable and ductile because layers of ions can slide over each other. Alloys are mixtures of metals with atoms of different sizes, which distort the layers and make the material harder.

## Key facts

- Ionic bonding involves electron transfer between a metal and a non-metal, forming oppositely charged ions.
- Ionic compounds have giant lattices, high melting points and conduct only when molten or dissolved.
- Covalent bonding involves shared electron pairs between non-metal atoms.
- Simple molecular substances have low melting points because their intermolecular forces are weak.
- Metallic bonding consists of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons, which explains conduction and malleability.
