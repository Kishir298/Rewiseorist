# The particle model

## Basic ideas

The **particle model** explains the properties of solids, liquids and gases by describing how their particles are arranged, how they move and how strongly they attract each other. All matter is made of tiny particles. The particles are always moving; the higher the temperature, the greater their average **kinetic energy**.

It is a simplification, but it explains density, changes of state, diffusion and gas pressure.

## The three states of matter

**Solids** have particles packed closely in a regular arrangement. They vibrate about fixed positions and have strong forces between them, giving a fixed shape and volume. Solids are difficult to compress.

**Liquids** have particles close together but randomly arranged. They can move past one another, so a liquid has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container. Liquids are difficult to compress.

**Gases** have particles far apart and randomly arranged. They move quickly in all directions with almost no forces between them. A gas fills its container and can be compressed easily.

## Changes of state

Heating a substance increases the kinetic energy of its particles. At the **melting point**, a solid becomes a liquid; at the **boiling point**, a liquid becomes a gas. Cooling reverses these changes: a gas **condenses** into a liquid and a liquid **freezes** into a solid. **Evaporation** happens at the surface of a liquid below its boiling point. **Sublimation** is when a solid changes directly into a gas.

During melting and boiling, the temperature stays constant even though energy is supplied. The energy overcomes forces between particles, increasing their potential energy rather than average kinetic energy. This is why heating curves have flat sections.

## Diffusion

**Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration, caused by their random motion. It is fastest in gases and slowest in solids.

Diffusion is faster at higher temperatures because particles have more kinetic energy and move faster. It is also faster when the particles are smaller or lighter. **Brownian motion** is the random movement of small particles suspended in a fluid, caused by collisions with the fluid's particles.

## Density

**Density** is mass per unit volume:

**density = mass ÷ volume**

It is measured in kg/m³ or g/cm³.

Worked example: a block has a mass of 200 g and a volume of 25 cm³.
Density = 200 ÷ 25 = 8 g/cm³.

Substances with particles packed more closely have a higher density. The density of an irregular object can be found by measuring its mass and the volume of water it displaces.

## Gas pressure

Gas pressure is caused by particles colliding with the walls of their container. Each collision exerts a small force. At a higher temperature, particles move faster and collide more often and harder, so pressure increases. Reducing the volume at constant temperature also increases pressure because collisions with the walls become more frequent.

## Key facts

- The particle model describes arrangement, movement and forces between particles in solids, liquids and gases.
- Solids have fixed shape and volume; liquids have fixed volume but flow; gases fill their container.
- Temperature stays constant during melting and boiling because energy overcomes forces between particles.
- Diffusion is the net movement from high to low concentration and is faster at higher temperatures.
- Density equals mass divided by volume; denser materials have particles packed more closely.
- Gas pressure is caused by particles colliding with the container walls.
