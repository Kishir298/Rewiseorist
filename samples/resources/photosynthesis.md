# Photosynthesis

## What photosynthesis is

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria use light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen. It is an **endothermic** reaction because it takes in energy, transferring energy from the light source to the chemical energy store of glucose.

Photosynthesis takes place mainly in the leaves, inside cells that contain **chloroplasts**. The green pigment **chlorophyll** inside chloroplasts absorbs light energy. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light most strongly and reflects green light, which is why most leaves look green.

## Word and balanced equations

Word equation:

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Balanced symbol equation:

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

The reaction needs light energy and chlorophyll, which are written above and below the arrow in a symbol equation.

## Limiting factors

A **limiting factor** is something that is in short supply and therefore limits the rate of photosynthesis. The main limiting factors are:

- **Light intensity:** more light gives more energy for the reaction, up to a point.
- **Carbon dioxide concentration:** carbon dioxide is a reactant, so more of it can increase the rate.
- **Temperature:** photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes, so the rate rises with temperature up to an optimum. Above the optimum, enzymes denature and the rate falls sharply.

On a graph, the rate often levels off. This happens because a different factor has become limiting: adding more light will not help if carbon dioxide is in short supply.

## Leaf adaptations

Leaves are adapted for efficient photosynthesis:

- They are broad and flat, giving a large surface area to absorb light.
- They are thin, so carbon dioxide and oxygen diffuse over short distances.
- The upper epidermis and cuticle are transparent, letting light reach the palisade cells.
- **Palisade mesophyll cells** near the top of the leaf contain many chloroplasts.
- **Stomata** in the lower epidermis allow carbon dioxide to diffuse in and oxygen to diffuse out. Guard cells open and close the stomata.
- **Xylem** brings water to the leaf, and **phloem** transports glucose away.
- Air spaces between spongy mesophyll cells help gases move through the leaf.

## Uses of glucose

The glucose made in photosynthesis is used for respiration to release energy. It can also be converted into starch for storage, cellulose for cell walls, amino acids when combined with nitrate ions, and oils or fats for storage in seeds.

## Required practical

In a common practical, pondweed is placed in water and a lamp is moved to different distances. The number of oxygen bubbles produced per minute, or the volume of oxygen collected, indicates the rate of photosynthesis. Temperature and carbon dioxide concentration must be controlled, and the pondweed should be left to settle before counting.

## Key facts

- Photosynthesis uses light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.
- The balanced equation is 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
- Chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light, mainly red and blue wavelengths.
- Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature can limit the rate.
- Leaves are adapted with a large surface area, thin shape, stomata and many chloroplasts.
- Glucose is used for respiration or stored as starch, cellulose, amino acids or oils.
